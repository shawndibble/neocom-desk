# Scope decisions — Threat counts a high kills share and recent losses; one request per zKillboard list

_Recorded 2026-10-08._

- **A high share of kills also makes a pilot Dangerous.** With 10 or more recent kills, a danger ratio of 50 or more still qualifies, and so does a kills share (`killerRatio`, kills over kills plus losses by ship count) of 60 or more. zKillboard's danger ratio weights each ship by size, so a heavy killer of small ships can sit near 40 on it while their record is two thirds kills. This refines `20261008-181210`, whose other rules stand.
- **A recent loss keeps a pilot out of Inactive.** A pilot with no kill in 90 days but a loss in that time is flying, so they read Low threat, with a sentence naming the losses. This narrows "Losses are not part of it" (`20261008-181210`): losses never raise a level above Low threat, they only stop the Inactive reading. Losses are not looked up for a pilot with a recent kill, who cannot be Inactive.
- **The loss dates come from the list Recent kills and losses already loads** (`fetchPilotKillmails`, the newest 25 kills and losses merged), not a new request. A recent loss of a pilot with no recent kill is always among those 25, since no kill can push it out.
- **Every reader shares one request per zKillboard list.** The kills list the kill history reads and the one Recent kills and losses reads is the same URL, so `fetchPilotList` (`src/lib/zkillboard.ts`) caches the request itself, in flight and for ten minutes, and a failure is never kept. A profile makes three zKillboard requests (stats, kills, losses) where it made four, and the Local list's loss lookup for a pilot whose kills it already read is a single losses request.
- **The Local list reads the extras only where they can change the level.** `PilotListRow.extras` holds the two ratios for a pilot with 10 or more recent kills and the loss dates for one with none, looked up in the same concurrency slot as their kills; everyone else stays idle and costs no extra request.
- **A pilot with no recent kill whose losses could not be read or dated gets no verdict.** If the kills-and-losses list fails, or any loss in it comes without a date, "Inactive" would be a guess, and a wrong one for a pilot who just lost a ship. The profile hides the badge and the band and says zKillboard's recent losses could not be read; a Local list row shows no badge. A pilot with a recent kill is unaffected, since the losses never decide their level.
- **The shared list cache is bounded.** Each cached list can hold 200 full killmail bodies, so `fetchPilotList` keeps at most 120 lists, dropping expired ones and then the oldest, rather than pinning a long Local list's worth for the session.
